  • Take with or after food to minimise transient GI side-effects.
  • Target dose 1–2 g/day (BMI/weight or appetite increases inform escalation).
  • Slow-release (MR) once daily may be used if GI side-effects persist or to support adherence.

Ongoing Monitoring Plan

  • Weight/BMI & Lactic Acidosis Screen: Dyspnoea, muscle cramps, abdominal pain, hypothermia, or asthenia.
  • Liver Function & HbA1c: Check annually.
  • Renal Function (eGFR): Annually Standard monitoring interval.
  • Vitamin B12: Annually Standard monitoring interval (treat deficiency per guidelines).
  • Sick Day Rule: Stop if systemically unwell with dehydration (severe diarrhoea/vomiting, fever, reduced fluid intake) or severe infection; restart when well.
  • Lactic Acidosis / Organ Failure: Stop immediately if lactic acidosis is suspected, or with acute alcohol intoxication, decompensated heart failure, respiratory failure, recent MI, or shock.
  • Iodinated Contrast Agents: Stop prior to or at administration; restart ≥48 hours later once renal function is stable.
  • Discontinuation Thresholds: Stop if eGFR <30 mL/min, if BMI <20 kg/m², or if the antipsychotic is stopped.

Mechanism & Counselling Pearls:

  • Lowers appetite, reduces hepatic glucose production, and improves insulin response.
  • Does not stimulate insulin secretion and therefore does not cause hypoglycaemia.
  • Low-cost, widely accessible medicine (continue lifestyle interventions alongside).
